# Raider Vocab

**Learn your class vocabulary list in about ten minutes a day.** A practice tool for Regis Jesuit
language classes (Spanish, French, Mandarin, Latin), built on the research on how people learn
words in a second language.

**Live:** https://rjedtech.github.io/Raider-Vocab/

One file. No server, no accounts, no build step. Everything a student types stays in their browser.

## For teachers

1. Open **For teachers**, name the list and pick the language.
2. Paste the words:
   - **From Quizlet:** the set's **… › Export**, keep "Tab" and "New line", copy, paste.
   - **From Excel:** copy the word and meaning columns. A third column of example sentences becomes
     fill-in-the-gap practice.
   - **By hand:** `la playa - the beach` or `la playa = the beach`, one per line.
   - **Mandarin:** characters, pinyin and English, in any order.
3. Press **Get the Canvas link**. You get the link and a ready-to-paste post telling students what to do.

The whole list travels inside the link, so there is nothing to sign into and nothing to expire.
`bonito/a`, `el/la estudiante` and `levantar(se)` accept every form they stand for; `;` separates two
different answers.

Options: accents must be exact (on by default, like a LockDown quiz), articles count, macrons
count (Latin), and how many new words a day.

**Progress codes.** Students can make a short code ("Show my teacher my progress") and paste it into
a Canvas assignment. Paste any number of codes into **Check progress codes** to see solid words and
days practised for each student. The code carries a light check so casual edits show up.

## For students

Open the link and press **Start today's practice**:

- **Meet** each new word: see it, hear it, say it. No typing yet.
- **Pick** its meaning, then later **type** it with the accents.
- **Come back tomorrow.** Words come back after 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days if you know them; tomorrow if
  you don't.

Other modes: **Meet the words**, **Type them all**, **Listen and type**, and **Quiz me like the real
quiz** (typed, accents count, results at the end). **My lists** mixes every saved list into one
review.

## Why it works this way

| Research finding | What the tool does |
|---|---|
| Retrieval beats re-reading (Karpicke & Roediger 2008; Barcroft 2007) | Every step asks before it shows |
| Spacing is the biggest lever in flashcard learning (Cepeda et al. 2006; Nakata & Webb 2016) | Day-spaced review; free practice never pushes a word out |
| Forced writing at first exposure can reduce learning (Barcroft 2004, 2006, 2007) | New words are seen, heard and said, not typed |
| Recognition helps first; productive recall builds spelling and use (Nakata 2013; Webb 2005, 2009) | Pick → type the meaning → type the word → hear it / use it in a sentence |
| Practice should match the test (Morris, Bransford & Franks 1977) | Typed answers, accents and articles count, a quiz-like round |
| Many voices and speaking rates help (Barcroft & Sommers 2005; Sommers & Barcroft 2007; Fichtner et al. 2025) | Rotates every voice for the language and varies the speed |
| Tight semantic sets interfere (Tinkham 1993, 1997; Erten & Tekin 2008) | Shuffling, new words in small groups, cross-list review |
| Keyword mnemonics fade without review (Wang, Thomas & Ouellette 1992) | A "memory hook" box only for words a student keeps missing |
| Deliberate study is one quarter of a balanced course (Nation 2007) | Short sessions; reading and listening are where fluency comes from |

Full write-up with links is on the **Why it works** tab.

## Answer checking

- **Spanish and French:** case and end punctuation ignored; `¿ ¡` optional; curly and straight
  apostrophes equal; `oe` accepted for `œ`. A missing accent, a missing or wrong article, or one
  wrong letter is "almost": the student sees which letter needs which mark and the US-International
  keys for it, types it once correctly, and the word comes back later in the session.
- **Mandarin:** pinyin with tone numbers (`ni3 hao3`) or tone marks, or characters. Right syllables
  with the wrong or missing tones is "almost", and says which tones.
- **Latin:** macrons optional unless the teacher turns them on. For principal parts and forms,
  use [Verba](https://rjedtech.github.io/Verba/).
- **English meanings:** "to", "the", "a" and notes in brackets ignored; any one of several listed
  meanings counts; one-letter typos count. If a student is sure they were right, "I was right. Count
  it." trusts them.

## Privacy

Lists, progress and names stay in the browser's local storage. The only outside request is the
anonymous GoatCounter page counter, which records the page path, never the `#list=` part of the
address.
